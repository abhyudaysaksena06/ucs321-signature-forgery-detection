01

// lesson 01 — the trap

A perfect score that means nothing.

Before training anything, measure the data. Every signature has a background: the paper as the scanner saw it. Drag the line and try a rule so simple it never looks at the handwriting.

If the page is brighter than , call it forged.

02

// lesson 02 — remove the shortcut

Take away the paper. Keep the pen.

Otsu's method picks the best ink threshold for each image, the signature is cropped, and every picture becomes a 96 × 192 mask. All backgrounds are now identical, so the only thing left to learn from is the stroke.

03

// lesson 03 — test on strangers

Who the model has met changes the score.

Shuffle images at random and the same person lands in both train and test. The model recognises the person, not the forgery. Splitting by signer means test people are complete strangers, which is the job in real life.

04

// lesson 04 — ask the right question

Not “is this fake?” but “is this theirs?”

A bank never judges a signature alone. It compares against a specimen on file. The Reference CNN does the same: it gets a genuine signature and the one in question as a two-channel image.
